Usage & Configuration

The Configuration File (config.toml)

Mission parameters are set in a TOML file, and config.toml documents the safe interval of each key inline. validate_config enforces them at startup: code-breaking values abort with a precise message, suspicious ones warn. Type mismatches (quoted numbers, float counts) and malformed TOML abort with a [CONFIG] message rather than a stack trace.

The keys the sections below do not discuss, with the value that applies when a key is absent:

Storage is checked before the run: check_storage_limits prints a per-artifact estimate derived from the configuration (payload, metadata, event logs, metrics, masks, plots, logs, file count) and enforces the [storage] budgets. With [retention] disabled, a projected overrun aborts before any directory is created. With the retention custodian enabled, the run proceeds with a warning while the steady-state footprint fits the budget: the non-prunable classes plus the delivered payload capped at high_watermark_gb. The custodian prunes delivered ground/<batch>/seg_*.csv payload copies, oldest ingested first and each at least grace_hours of mission time after its ingestion, and records each pruning as a pruned event. Event logs, metrics, masks, snapshots, and lost/ are kept. Log files rotate at log_rotate_mb whether or not retention is enabled.

Mission Clock

simulation.mode selects the clock the mission runs on (Mission Clocks: Wall and Tick). "wall", the default, paces mission time against the wall clock and reads speed_up and mission_wall_seconds; "tick" advances mission time logically from one component wakeup to the next and reads mission_days instead. The two key sets are disjoint: a speed_up or mission_wall_seconds in tick mode, or a mission_days in wall mode, is a configuration error naming the mode, and tick mode also rejects dashboard.receiver_status_panel = true. scenarios/deterministic_1d.toml is the library's tick scenario, one mission day. Any wall scenario runs on the tick clock with its span unchanged through --tick on the command line (TelemetryCore.tick_configuration in a session); the figures of this manual are made that way.

Scenario Library

The repository ships a library of complete configurations under scenarios/, each runnable by path; scenarios/README.md carries the coverage matrix and the expected regime of every file. config.toml at the package root is the default entry point and is a copy of scenarios/recovery_12h_seasonal.toml. The TOML comments give each key's meaning, admissible choices, and safe interval; the scenario rationale is in the library README and below.

The reference scenario, recovery at the seasonal peak, spans 7 mission days from 30 March 2035 in about 3 min of wall time. It runs the physical link, 230 kbit/s downlink against 75 kbit/s production, flat within the pass (the Definition Study Report's sustained rate). The 4-hour seasonal extension, evaluated on the date of each pass, widens the daily 8-hour window to 12-hour passes: 30 March is the peak day, and the extension stays within minutes of its maximum over the week. The mission opens with a 2-day blind-spot backlog (288 batches at the shipped physics: 60 s segments, 10 segments per batch → 144 batches/day) served by 12-hour passes of about 210 batches each over a bursty Gilbert–Elliott channel (sticky BAD state, 50 % loss while BAD). Three scheduled events stress the mission: a 15-minute antenna repointing on day 1.5 (a generation gap), a day-2.5 solar-flare-class full blackout (18 h, then a 12 h linear recovery ramp with 5× elevated loss), and a day-5.0 partial DSN outage (severity 0.8, 12 h, 6 h ramp, 3× loss). An event marker at 14:00 on day 4 triggers, six hours later, a 3-hour low-latency period at half capacity.

scenarios/stress_8h_bursty.toml runs the same disruption timeline on plain 8-hour passes with the seasonal term disabled: the link delivers 138 batches per day against 144 produced, and the backlog grows. scenarios/nominal_8h.toml is the balanced case without backlog or disruptions, and scenarios/abstraction_gaussian_peak.toml the peak-capacity abstraction, a 60 batches/h peak under a Gaussian profile. The library also covers a drop policy, an explicit pass schedule, a 30-day seasonal mission reaching the recorder ceiling, the coarse-batch regime of 2400 s segments, external ingestion, and one mission day on the tick clock.

Under the physical rate pair and 8-hour passes only the flat profile sustains production. A day's capacity is the link rate times the pass length times the profile mean: flat 1.0, sigmoid with k = 10: 0.93, sine 0.5, Gaussian with σ = 0.15: 0.38. The shaped profiles are stress abstractions of a partially usable pass. validate_config warns when the rate pair meets a shaped profile, stating the profile mean and the capacity of one nominal pass against the daily production (TelemetryCore.capacity_balance), and the mission banner prints the same balance as its Capacity: line for every run.

Every scenario without a seasonal term starts on 1 January 2035, the calendar epoch of the ground-segment conventions, where the date labels the time axis and does not act on the passes. In the two seasonal scenarios the start date sets the phase of the extension, which is then evaluated on the calendar date of each pass: a mission spanning months sees its passes lengthen and shorten with the season (Contact Schedule and Low-Latency Periods).

The arguments are optional and order-independent. One ending in .toml selects the configuration (a relative path resolves against the current directory, then the package root), --tick runs it on the tick clock, and any other argument sets the run ID:

julia scripts/run_full_sim.jl MY_RUN scenarios/stress_8h_bursty.toml

Each run archives the configuration it used as config_snapshot.toml, and post-processing reads that snapshot.

Packet Loss & Disruptions

[packet_loss]
enabled = true
model = "gilbert_elliott"   # or "bernoulli" (then set p_loss)
p_good_to_bad = 0.02
p_bad_to_good = 0.30
p_loss_good = 0.005
p_loss_bad = 0.40
on_loss = "retransmit"      # or "drop" (= lose on first failure)
max_retries = 3

[[disruption.events]]
type = "link_disruption"    # free string
label = "solar flare"       # optional display name on the dashboard status line
start_day = 10.0            # mission days after start_sim_time
duration_hours = 60.0       # full-blackout phase
severity = 1.0              # 1.0 = zero capacity; 0.4 = 60% capacity retained
recovery_hours = 12.0       # linear ramp back to nominal
loss_multiplier = 8.0       # stochastic loss × 8 during blackout + ramp

[[disruption.events]]
type = "antenna_repointing" # affects = "generation" by default for this type
start_day = 1.5
duration_hours = 0.25       # no data produced for 15 min; severity/recovery/loss ignored

The on-board recorder is bounded by [telemetry] onboard_capacity_days (default 14): once the buffer holds that many days of production, new batches are discarded until room returns (no eviction), and the loss is recorded as a RECORDER gap in the transmit event log.

simulation.rng_seed seeds the packet-loss channel, the run's only random stream; the synthetic payload is declared by the event markers and involves no draw. The same seed gives the same record on either clock (Mission Clocks: Wall and Tick).

Contact Schedule

[contacts] shapes the daily window of [telemetry]; every key is optional.

[contacts]
seasonal_extension_hours = 4.0      # evaluated on each pass's date: 8 h at the trough, 12 h at the peak
season_peak_day_of_year = 89.0
low_latency_enabled = true          # set to false for the counterfactual run

[[contacts.exceptions]]             # the window of one date, as given
date = "2035-01-04"
duration_hours = 0.0                # 0 = missed pass; omit start to keep session_start

[[contacts.low_latency_periods]]
start = "2035-01-05T20:00:00"
duration_hours = 3.0
capacity_fraction = 0.5             # station availability as a fraction of peak
label = "transient follow-up"

A planned pass list replaces the daily generator (exceptions are then not admissible), either inline

[[contacts.passes]]
start = "2035-01-02T08:00:00"
duration_hours = 6.0

or as schedule_csv = "path/to/passes.csv" with the columns Start, DurationHours (relative paths resolve against the current directory, then the package root). Passes may cross midnight and must not overlap.

Event Markers

[[events.markers]] declare the instants the alert-latency metric is evaluated at; a marker may trigger a low-latency period after a reaction delay, and [ground] holds the processing budget added to every latency.

[ground]
processing_latency_hours = 1.0

[[events.markers]]
time = "2035-01-05T14:00:00"
label = "transient candidate"
low_latency_after_hours = 6.0       # optional: triggered period, 6 h after the marker
low_latency_duration_hours = 3.0
low_latency_capacity_fraction = 0.5

Label Spans

[[labels]] entries declare labeled spans of payload rows, such as the samples of an injected event that a payload exporter records beside the series it writes. The run writes them to label_spans.csv with the other labeled intervals of the scenario (disruption phases, generation gaps, contact passes, low-latency periods, the initial downtime), where a consumer reads them. A [payload] section, the provenance a payload exporter records with its scenario fragment, is carried into the snapshot unchecked.

[[labels]]
label = "mbhb_1"
label_start_index = 100   # payload rows, inclusive; start > end declares an empty span
label_end_index = 2500

Synthetic Payload

With data_source = "synthetic" the payload is a binary flag series: a segment is 1 throughout when its content span holds the instant of an [[events.markers]] entry and 0 throughout otherwise. segment_duration_sec must be a whole number of milliseconds and hold at least one sample at sample_rate.

External Data Ingestion

An external high-frequency CSV time series replaces the synthetic payload with:

[physics]
data_source = "external"
external_data_path = "path/to/data.csv"
external_data_columns = []    # or ["A", "E"]: the file's columns to ingest, in order
sample_rate = 1024.0
segment_duration_sec = 60.0
batch_size = 15

Row r of the series is the sample at start_sim_time − initial_downtime_days · 86 400 s + (r − 1) / sample_rate: the first rows fill the pre-existing blind-spot backlog, a generation gap skips the rows of its interval, and past the last row the payload is zero. Each batch records the row of its first sample (payload_row, Batch Identity → Sample Interval).

A file may carry several columns. external_data_columns names those to ingest, in that order; left empty, the Amplitude column is taken, or else the first column, under the name Amplitude. The ingested columns form one payload and share each batch, transfer and loss draw. Each segment file carries one column per payload column under its name. The names are stamped into the run's provenance and descriptor, and the adapter reads one column (read_batch) or all of them (read_batch_matrix). A listed column absent from the file's header is rejected at validation.

Running the Simulation

The emitter and the receiver run as cooperative tasks on one thread in both modes, so Julia needs no thread option.

Interactive Dashboard:

julia scripts/launch_dashboard.jl

This spawns the log terminals and the UnicodePlots live viewer, which redraws only on a state change, for each [dashboard] flag open_* set to true; the shipped configurations set them false.

Headless Mode:

julia scripts/run_full_sim.jl
julia scripts/run_full_sim.jl --tick MY_RUN scenarios/stress_8h_bursty.toml

The arguments follow the rule given under Scenario Library. The receiver's console status panel (a text panel redrawn on every receiver iteration, which clears the terminal) is off by default; dashboard.receiver_status_panel = true enables it for a headless run whose terminal shows nothing else.

Ensembles and Sweeps

A sweep runs one configuration on the tick clock once per seed, with optional overrides of any key, and gathers the realized totals of every run into one table. Any run of the table can be regenerated exactly from its seed and the sweep's provenance:

julia scripts/run_sweep.jl scenarios/stress_8h_bursty.toml MY_SWEEP --seeds 1:8 --workers 4 --set packet_loss.p_loss_bad=0.6

--seeds takes a range or a comma-separated list. --workers N runs N members at once as tasks on N Julia threads (serial without it); the script restarts itself with --threads=N when Julia was started with fewer. On the tick clock the runs are independent of the host's load and share no state, so parallel runs reproduce the serial ones exactly. Every --set section.key=value applies to all runs; the value is read as a boolean, an integer, a float, or else a string (quotes optional). The runs land under data/runs/<PREFIX>_seed<seed>/, and their post-processing writes the gap table and the metrology tables but no figures and no mask timeline. data/sweeps/<PREFIX>/ receives summary.csv, one row per run with the seed, the generated and delivered counts, live and archive, lost, failed transfers (column retries), the buffer and link at the end, the share within the delivery requirement, the alert-latency medians at 24 h and 72 h under the realized doctrine and the FIFO drain, and the hole count. Beside it, sweep.toml records the sweep's provenance: the base configuration's hash, the overrides, the seeds, the commit and version, the date. In a session, Sweeps.run_sweep(cfg; prefix, seeds, overrides, workers) returns the table and the directory, and Sweeps.summarize_run(run_dir) reads one run's totals.

Post-Processing & Masks

After a run, post-processing writes masks/telemetry_mask_timeline.csv, a 2D matrix of the state of every batch (0=Future, 1=Onboard, 2=Link, 3=Ground, 4=Lost) at every telemetry event. The reconstruction is exact: the emitter and receiver append every batch milestone to events_tx.csv / events_rx.csv (generation, transmission, ingest, retry, loss), and post-processing replays those ground-truth logs.

A run whose config_snapshot.toml is missing or unparsable is refused with a [CONFIG] error.

Before any figure, post-processing writes the gap table masks/gaps.csv. It lists every hole of the delivered record as a payload-row interval with its cause: a lost or pruned batch, a batch undelivered at run end, a generation gap by type (stream, scheduled, recorder), or the unreached tail of an external record. A gap-aware analysis takes it as its declared input (Stationary Analysis), and Masks.gap_table(run_dir) computes it for a run made before the product existed. The declared-hole feed holes.csv is appended during the run as each hole is declared. Post-processing settles it from the gap table, appending the end-of-run holes with the nominal mission end as their declaration instant, after which the feed and the table agree. A row of the feed that the table does not derive is reported as a warning in the run log (Masks.hole_feed_excess); the table is the reference. The label-span table label_spans.csv is written at mission start and regenerated here only when a run lacks it. Last, post-processing writes descriptor.toml, the run's identity for a downstream collection: producer version and source identity (tree hash and commit, also for a copy installed by Pkg), configuration hash, seed, clock mode, payload geometry, and the size and SHA-256 of every table present.

With hdf5_export = true in [post_processing] every table product (event logs, hole feed, metrics, mask timeline, batch epochs, gap table, metrology tables, markers, label spans, component events) is additionally written to products.h5 with the run's provenance as attributes (HDF5 Product Export); the same file can be produced afterward for any run:

julia scripts/postprocessing/export_hdf5.jl [RUN_ID]

Publication Figures

[post_processing.publication] re-renders the figures of a run (mission summary, session figures, alert latency, delivery delay, batch-state raster) at a declared printed width in a vector format, into one directory with a PROVENANCE.toml sidecar (run ID, package version, git commit, snapshot hash, file list), each file named <stem>__<run_id>.<format>:

[post_processing.publication]
enabled = true
format = "pdf"              # or "svg"
column_width_mm = 120.0     # printed width [mm], 100 to 400; default 178
export_dir = "figures_export"     # "" = <run_dir>/publication; relative to the current directory

Every figure is composed at one standard layout (1200 Makie units wide, 26-unit type) and the export scales it as a whole, so a narrower figure is a miniature of the standard one: at 178 mm the axis labels print at about 11 pt and the tick labels at about 9 pt, at the 100 mm floor at about 6 pt and 5 pt. Figure heights and legend rows follow from Makie's own layout measurements at the standard layout. In-axis annotations move to whichever end of the axis the disruption rules and event markers leave free, and the delivery-delay requirement rule stops above its annotation block. The same export runs afterward for any run with the settings of its snapshot:

julia scripts/postprocessing/export_publication_figures.jl [RUN_ID]

Session Figures of Long Missions

Each contact window, nominal pass or low-latency period, receives a session figure (session_day<kk>_detail.png, session_day<kk>_low_latency_detail.png), so their number grows with the mission: a year of daily passes is 365 figures, about a second and 0.6 MB each. Two keys bound the stage:

[post_processing]
session_figures = true      # false: the mission summary only
session_figures_max = 0     # at most this many, evenly spaced over the contacts; 0 = no cap

Under a cap the windows are spread evenly over the mission, the first and the last included. The keys are read from the run's own snapshot, so the publication export and a later call of MissionFigures.generate_mission_plots select the same windows.

The stage holds one figure in memory at a time: each figure is drawn in a theme scope of its own and released after it is saved, so its memory does not grow with the number of windows. The start-up estimate prints it as Figure RAM and checks it against storage.max_ram_gb, beside the replay RAM of the mask timeline.

Above 120 mission days the capacity panel of the mission summary shows the mean capacity of each mission day, and its legend says so; passes drawn one by one would merge into a solid block. An 8-hour pass then reads as a third of the day, a missed pass as zero, and the seasonal extension as the slow rise and fall of that level. Count axes that reach 10⁴ batches carry tick labels with one common power of ten (10⁴, 2×10⁴, …).

The post-processing writes its products in order of cost: the gap table, the settled hole feed and the label spans, the metrology tables, then the alert-latency and delivery-delay figures, the mask timeline and the raster drawn from it, the mission summary and the session figures, the exports, and the run descriptor last. A run whose supervisor process was killed during that stage is completed with scripts/postprocessing/complete_run.jl (Availability Window & Lifecycle Sentinels).

Batch-State Raster

One further figure follows every run, behind its own flag:

[post_processing]
state_raster = true         # the batch-state timeline as a raster

state_raster.png draws masks/telemetry_mask_timeline.csv with one column per batch, one row per recorded event, and one color per state, in the live and archive colors of every other figure. The Scenario Gallery shows how to read it. It needs generate_mask_timeline, and is skipped without it.

GIF Animation

To visualize the LIFO/FIFO routing physics of a completed run:

julia scripts/postprocessing/generate_gif.jl [--web] [RUN_ID]

Each frame is one row of mission_profile.csv and states the mission clock, the link state, and the buffer counters. The rows follow a change-driven cadence, so the animation is not linear in mission time. Two rendering profiles exist: the default archive profile (1400 units wide, two raster units each, at most 800 frames) and --web, which writes the size the README and the manual carry (900 px, at most 240 frames, a few megabytes) as telemetry_animation_web.gif.
